# Creative rules

Every draft follows these rules. They come from real mistakes in earlier AI reels. Rules marked MUST are strict:
a draft that breaks one is not finished. The Creator step sends this file with every "needs_text"; the self-check
uses section 6.

## 1. Write strong prompts
A weak prompt gives a generic, plastic, "AI-looking" picture. A strong prompt gives one you would stop scrolling for.
Every image prompt and every scene `visual` MUST say, in this order:
1. **Subject**: exactly what we see, with real details. Not "a cake" but "a two-layer dark chocolate cake with glossy
   ganache dripping down the sides, one slice cut out showing moist layers and chocolate cream filling".
2. **Action**: what is happening at this moment (steam rising, a hand dusting cocoa, a customer laughing mid-bite).
3. **Setting**: the real place of this business, with its layout (a small bakery counter of light wood, glass display
   case on the left, brick wall behind, morning light from a window on the right).
4. **Light**: the kind and direction (soft morning window light from the right, warm golden hour, bright clean daylight).
5. **Camera**: shot size and angle (close-up at table height, overhead flat lay, medium shot at eye level), lens feel
   (shallow depth of field, background softly blurred).
6. **Mood and style**: one clear look (warm, appetizing, editorial food photography; or bright, clean, modern
   product photography). Keep the same style in every image of a post and every scene of a reel.
7. **Textures and small real details**: crumbs, condensation, fabric weave, wood grain, fingerprints on glass. These
   make a picture feel real.
8. **Exclusions**: "no text, no letters, no logos, no watermark" (unless the post asks for text, section 2).

Use concrete nouns and adjectives, never vague ones ("beautiful", "amazing", "nice"). One main subject per picture.
Use the brand's colors from the brand brief in props and background when they fit.

## 2. Images
- No text, letters, numbers or logos in AI images. Captions are added separately. For shop fronts, papers, menus,
  boxes and screens say "no signs, no lettering and no words anywhere". Stacks of paper are "plain blank pages".
- When the owner wants text in the picture: put it in the prompt in quotes, short (2 to 6 words), and say
  "spelled exactly, large, clear sans-serif font". Check the spelling in the self-check.
- The product MUST look like the real product. When the owner has given photos (the `owner` folder), use them
  instead of AI images. Never invent a product, flavour, menu item or package the business does not sell.
- Real-world physics and scale: people, furniture, doors, food and cars keep their true size. Nothing floats.
- Every person looks different (face, hair, skin, build, clothes). Never a crowd of the same face.
  Real, named people are never drawn realistically.
- Faces look natural: no wide cartoon grins, no mouths full of food in a calm moment.
- Screens face the person using them. Show a screen's contents over the person's shoulder or as a close-up.
  App buttons and notifications appear only on a screen, never floating in the air.
- Hands: say how many and where ("her right hand holds the knife, her left hand rests flat on the board"). Extra
  hands and fingers are the most common error.
- Keep the main subject in the middle of the picture. In 9:16, keep important things out of the top 10%, the
  bottom 15% and the right edge from mid-height down (the app's buttons and caption sit there).
- Period and place: no object, building or clothing that does not fit the business's place and time.

## 3. Reels and videos
Each scene has two parts:
- `visual`: the FIRST frame of the scene (section 1 rules). The clip starts from this picture.
- `motion`: only what changes during the clip, with strong verbs (pours, slices, lifts, turns, steps), and that the
  movement goes on for the whole clip.

Rules:
- The first scene (the hook) MUST be the most eye-catching shot of the post: the finished product, the surprise,
  the result. Never a logo, an empty room or a slow setup.
- People are already in the middle of their action at the first frame, facing their task. They MUST NOT look at the
  camera unless the scene says so.
- One clip = one simple action. A multi-step action (open the box, take out the cake, put it on the plate) is split
  into two or three scenes. The video model reaches the end state only at the last moment.
- Cause before effect: a hand cuts, then the slice comes out. Nothing opens, moves or changes by itself.
- The action only moves forward: anything cut, poured, opened or placed stays that way. Nothing reverses or resets.
- Solid things never pass through each other. People stand clear of anything that moves.
- Small props morph when moved around: keep them still, or in one hand with one simple shape. No two people's hands
  on one object; arms never cross.
- Fine hand work (icing, pouring, cutting): say "locked-off camera, no camera movement".
- Camera moves stay simple and slow: push in, pull back, slow orbit. No long moves across patterns or shelves.
- Avoid doors opening on screen; show the result instead (the customer already inside).
- Same place in two scenes: repeat the place's layout words exactly (what is behind, left and right), so it looks
  the same. Same person in two scenes: repeat their look word for word (face, hair, clothes).
- Light matches the time of the scene in every shot of it.
- Each scene is 2 to 6 seconds of voice. A line with two ideas gets two scenes.

## 4. Voice and words
- The voiceover is read in one take. Write full, plain spoken sentences, never fragments.
- Hook: the most surprising or tempting fact in the first sentence, simple enough to get on first hearing.
- Every "it", "they" and "this" points to one clear thing. Never start two lines in a row with the same words.
- Names, prices and numbers the voice may misread get a `say` spelling with the same number of words as the line's
  words they replace ("Rs 499" -> "four ninety-nine rupees" is fine in `say`; captions show `voiceover`).
- End with a clear call to action (visit, order, comment), in the brand voice.

## 5. Facts and brand
- MUST use only facts from the brand brief, the script and the owner's words: no invented prices, dates, offers,
  ingredients, awards or reviews.
- Never use the brief's banned topics. Never copy another account's words, pictures or music.
- Follow the owner's change notes exactly; change nothing else.

## 6. Self-check (before the owner sees anything)
Open EVERY file the Creator lists for review and answer for each one; write pass or fail with what you saw:
1. Does it show the post's subject clearly, and look like the real product and place?
2. Is it free of text, letters and logos (or, when text was asked, is it spelled exactly)?
3. Are the hands, fingers, faces and bodies normal? Is anyone duplicated, merged or floating?
4. Is the scale real, and does nothing break physics?
5. Is the important part clear of the app overlay areas (section 2)?
6. Reel frame sheets: does the action move forward with no morphing, no person turning into another, no prop
   appearing from nowhere, nobody looking at the camera, no black bars, and light that matches the next scene?
7. Voice check: is every script word heard? Spelling-only differences are fine; a missing or changed word fails.
8. Is it strong enough to stop a scroll? A dull, flat or generic picture fails: rewrite the prompt (section 1).
A failed item gets a better prompt and is made again (only that item is paid for). At most 2 fix rounds; list
anything still doubtful for the owner.
